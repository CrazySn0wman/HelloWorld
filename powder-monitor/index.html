How to read powder around Salzburg

Storm direction decides who gets the snow

  • Nordstau (NW–N flow): moist air piles up against the northern rim of the Alps. Fieberbrunn, Lofer, Hochkönig, Tennengebirge, Dachstein and Feuerkogel get the most. Inner valleys such as Großarl often stay dry.
  • Südstau (S–SW flow): the snow falls south of the main ridge: Lungau, Katschberg, Sportgastein and the high Tauern. The north side often gets Föhn and nothing.
  • Westerly flow: spreads snow more evenly, usually more on the north side.

What makes a good powder day

  • Fresh snow: 20 cm+ in the last 48 hours is a real powder day; 10 cm is a nice top-up.
  • Cold: below about −3 °C at the top, the snow stays light and dry. Above 0 °C it gets heavy fast.
  • Little wind: strong wind strips ridges, packs the snow and builds slabs in the lee.
  • Day after the storm: clearing skies after a snowfall overnight are the jackpot.
  • Altitude: when the snow line is high, only the Kitzsteinhorn, Sportgastein and Obertauern get dry snow.

Before you leave the piste

This page is a weather screen, not an avalanche forecast. Fresh snow plus wind is exactly when avalanche danger rises. Check the official bulletin every time, carry transceiver, shovel and probe, and use a local guide in terrain you don't know (strongly recommended at Krippenstein).

  • lawine.at: all Austrian avalanche warning services
  • avalanche.report: Tirol, South Tyrol, Trentino

How the score works

For each day: snow from the previous two days and that day itself (weighted toward the most recent), plus a bonus for cold and clear skies, minus wind. Areas with more freeride terrain get a small bonus once there is real fresh snow. 10 = go now; 6+ = worth the drive; under 3 = groomers.

Forecasts are model output at about the upper station's height. They're good for spotting storms, less accurate for exact centimetres. Drive times are rough estimates for normal traffic.
